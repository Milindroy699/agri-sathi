## Overview

Soybean is central India's main kharif oilseed and the backbone of the farm economy across
the Malwa plateau of Madhya Pradesh and neighbouring Maharashtra and Rajasthan. It is a
short, mostly rain-fed crop — in the field for only about three months — which makes
**timely sowing** its single biggest lever: sow in the first fortnight of the monsoon, once
75–100 mm of rain has wet the soil to depth, and the crop escapes both late-season disease
and the worst of the whitefly that carries yellow mosaic virus.

Because it is a legume, soybean **makes most of its own nitrogen** — but only if the
**Rhizobium seed inoculant is alive and applied correctly**: added last (after any
fungicide), kept out of the sun, and sown within a few hours. A failed inoculation is one of
the most common reasons for a pale, disappointing crop. The other essential input is
**sulphur**, which an oilseed needs and which single super phosphate conveniently supplies.

Two field risks define the season: **drainage** (soybean will not survive even a day of
standing water, so open channels before the rain) and **defoliating caterpillars** at and
after flowering, which should be managed with traps, hand-collection and biologicals first,
and selective sprays only once they cross threshold.
