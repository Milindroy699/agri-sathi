## Overview

Mustard is India's main rabi oilseed and the dominant winter crop of the drier tracts of
Rajasthan, Haryana, western Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh. It is well suited to those
areas because it needs **little water** — often just two irrigations — and tolerates mild
salinity and alkalinity better than wheat or gram.

As with wheat, **sowing date is the master variable**: mustard sown by the end of October
flowers in the mild part of winter and fills its pods before the March heat, while late-sown
crops run into two problems at once — the **January aphid peak** hitting the crop at
flowering, and hot winds during pod fill. Early-maturing varieties exist specifically to let
farmers sow late (after paddy or cotton) and still escape both.

Two inputs matter more for mustard than for most crops. **Sulphur** — 40 kg/ha, usually via
gypsum or single super phosphate — because mustard is exceptionally sulphur-hungry and
sulphur directly drives oil content. And **timely aphid management** at flowering: scouting
twice a week from the flower-bud stage, clipping the first infested shoot tips, protecting
the strong natural enemy complex, and spraying — late in the afternoon to spare bees — only
when colonies cross threshold.
