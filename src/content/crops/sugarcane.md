## Overview

Sugarcane is a year-long crop — planted once and standing in the field for 10 to 18 months —
so a mistake at planting is a mistake you live with for a whole year. It is also the most
water-hungry major crop in India, which makes it best suited to assured-irrigation belts
like western Uttar Pradesh and the canal and well commands of Maharashtra and the south.

The calendar on this page counts from planting. After the main "plant crop", most farmers
take one or two **ratoon crops** — letting the stubble resprout — which saves the cost of
seed and planting but yields less each time and accumulates pests and disease. Replant with
fresh, certified seed after two or three ratoons at most.

Three things decide a sugarcane crop. **Seed** — use young (8–10 month), disease-free cane
from a proper three-tier nursery, because red rot and wilt travel in the seed. **Water in
the formative and grand-growth stages** — the crop must never wilt from about 45 to 270
days, as lost tillers and shortened internodes never recover. And **earthing up and
propping** — a heavy cane crop that is not hilled up and tied will lodge in the monsoon wind
and lose both tonnage and sugar. Across north India, **red rot breaking the resistance of
the once-dominant Co 0238** now makes timely variety change the single most important
decision.
