## Overview

Chickpea — gram, chana — is India's most important pulse and the country's main source of
dal protein. It is a hardy, low-input rabi crop that is usually grown **rain-fed on the
moisture left in the soil after the monsoon**, with no irrigation at all, which is why it
fits the black-soil tracts of Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra so well.

The management of chickpea is mostly about **restraint**. It fixes its own nitrogen, so
never top-dress urea — extra nitrogen just produces tall leafy plants that flower heavily
and set few pods. It does not want much water — at most one or two light irrigations, and
none at peak flowering. And it is best sown a little **late and deep** into cooler soil,
because the two things that most damage it — **Fusarium wilt** and **dry root rot** — are
both worse in warm soil.

Two decisions carry the crop. **Variety** — grow a wilt-resistant one (JG 11 and its
relatives), because the wilt fungus lives in the soil for years and there is no cure once a
plant is infected. And **pod borer management** — Helicoverpa is the single biggest threat
to any Indian pulse. Start early at the flower-bud stage with pheromone traps, bird perches,
and HaNPV or neem on the small larvae; keep sprays for when the pest crosses threshold, and
rotate insecticide groups every time.
